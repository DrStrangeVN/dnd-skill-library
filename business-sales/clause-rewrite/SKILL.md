# Clause Rewrite

Revise only the scope the user asked for. Keep the user's commercial objective and the rest of the agreement in view.

## Workflow

1. Read the clause and surrounding definitions, cross-references, schedules, and relevant provisions. Do not review a clause in isolation when its meaning depends on other text.
2. Confirm which party the user represents and the desired negotiating position. If unknown, show a balanced draft or ask when the choice changes material rights.
3. Identify the problem: ambiguity, missing process, uneven risk, inconsistent terms, or legal-source question. Verify current law through `law-source-check` before stating a legal requirement.
4. Provide: (a) original text or short excerpt, (b) proposed text, (c) changes and rationale, (d) fallback wording or open decisions where useful.
5. Use placeholders for unknown facts. Define trigger, timeline, notice method, evidence, cure period, cost, consequences, and exceptions when relevant.
6. Check that the revised clause fits defined terms and does not conflict with termination, liability, payment, dispute, or order-of-precedence provisions.

## Drafting rules

- Preserve original legal and business intent unless the user explicitly asks to change it.
- Do not state that draft language is legally compliant, enforceable, or negotiation-proof.
- Do not add a deadline, cap, warranty, penalty, exclusivity, indemnity, waiver, or remedy as if agreed. Label it proposed and show options.
- Separate legal minimums from favorable negotiating terms.
- For bilingual contracts, do not choose a controlling language without instruction; identify inconsistencies across versions.
- Avoid changing unrelated provisions. List consequential changes clearly.

## Document edits

When editing an uploaded Word file, work on a copy. Preserve headings, numbering, tables, defined-term capitalization, and formatting where possible. If true tracked changes are unavailable, do not claim that they are tracked: provide a marked-up copy using visible annotations or a change log, plus a clean copy only when requested.

## Output

Return the revised clause in copy-ready form, with a concise explanation and decision points. For document-wide edits, provide an issue log and identify each edited section.
