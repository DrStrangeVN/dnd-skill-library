# Contract Document

Draft for the user's stated commercial objective, using verified facts and the applicable law sources. The result is a working draft for review, not a lawyer's opinion or a guarantee of validity or enforceability.

## Before drafting

Identify the agreement type, the user's party, counterparty, scope, value, dates, payment, delivery/performance, rights to protect, governing law, and special approvals. Use information supplied in the conversation and available source documents. Ask for material missing terms that change the bargain; otherwise use clearly marked `[CẦN ĐIỀN]` placeholders. Never invent the counterparty's corporate details, transaction price, legal authority, negotiated terms, or signatures.

For Danang Dragons, read `references/danang-dragons-party-profile.md` and `references/danang-dragons-rights-playbook.md`. Insert the company block exactly as supplied, unless the user gives a corrected version. Label Ms. Bùi Thị Minh Nguyệt as an authorized representative under the referenced authorization, not as the company's legal representative unless verified. Check the authorization's current validity, scope, term, parties, transaction type, and any limit before preparing a signature-ready version. If the authorization itself is not supplied, show a verification note rather than claiming it was checked.

## Drafting workflow

1. Summarize the proposed deal and list assumptions/placeholders.
2. Check relevant Vietnamese legal sources via `law-source-check`; determine whether sector-specific, consumer, employment, personal-data, IP, foreign-law, or dispute rules are relevant.
3. Prepare the agreement with definitions, scope, deliverables, responsibilities, acceptance, price/tax/payment, term, change control, confidentiality/data/IP, liability/remedies, force majeure, suspension/termination, transition, notices, dispute resolution, governing law, language priority, notices, appendices, and signing blocks as appropriate to the deal. Do not add irrelevant boilerplate mechanically.
4. Review the draft from Danang Dragons' perspective when it is the user's party. Explain trade-offs and provide fallback positions; do not make a one-sided clause sound legally mandatory when it is a negotiation choice.
5. Run a cross-reference, defined-term, date, amount, party identity, missing-field, and internal consistency check.
6. Create an editable `.docx` if document-generation tools are available in this session. If the session cannot produce files, return the complete Word-ready draft and state that a DOCX was not generated.
7. If producing a DOCX, render and visually inspect every page before delivery. Keep a copy of the user's original source; never overwrite it unless expressly requested.

## Word formatting

Use a formal, readable private-contract layout, not an official government-form claim:

- A4 portrait unless the tables require landscape.
- Times New Roman, 13 pt body text by default; readable line spacing around 1.15–1.3; consistent paragraph spacing.
- Normal margins around 2.5 cm; keep signature blocks together and avoid isolated headings at page bottoms.
- Center the contract title; use clear Article/Clause numbering (`ĐIỀU 1.`, `1.1`, `a)`) and consistent heading styles.
- Start with the contract number/date only if supplied; use `[SỐ HỢP ĐỒNG]` and `[NGÀY KÝ]` otherwise.
- Include party blocks, legal basis only when verified and relevant, body clauses, appendices, and clean signature blocks with no prefilled signature or seal.
- Add page numbering and a short document title in the footer when appropriate. Avoid decorative cover pages for a contract unless requested.
- Use tables sparingly for comparable schedules, deliverables, prices, or signatory blocks. Keep them editable and prevent row splitting when possible.
- Clearly distinguish `[CẦN ĐIỀN]`, `[CẦN XÁC NHẬN]`, proposed fallback language, and final agreed text.

The exact font, spacing, and layout are presentation choices. Do not claim that they are legally required. If the user supplies a template, follow it unless it conflicts with an explicit instruction.

## Rights-first review for Danang Dragons

Check whether the agreement clearly limits the grant of the club name/logo, team marks, uniform, player image/likeness, footage, venue access, sponsor category exclusivity, content usage, and sublicensing by medium, territory, duration, purpose, approval, and post-termination use. Confirm league, player, venue, and third-party permissions before promising rights the club may not control.

Also review deliverable counts and dates, sponsor/product claims, in-kind quantity/quality and delivery, payment milestones, VAT/tax treatment, acceptance/evidence, late payment, cure period, liability allocation, indemnity, IP ownership, confidentiality, data handling, cancellation/refund, event disruption, and dispute process. Treat these as negotiation checks; confirm law and contract context before proposing final text.

## Deliverable

Provide:

1. An assumptions/open-items list.
2. The editable DOCX draft.
3. A brief issues summary covering club rights, material commercial risks, authority to sign, and provisions needing lawyer/management approval.

Do not say “ready to sign” unless the user explicitly asks for a near-final check and all material facts, authority documents, approvals, legal sources, and annexes have been verified. Even then, recommend final counsel review for high-impact agreements.
