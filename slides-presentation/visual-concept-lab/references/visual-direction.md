# Visual direction — soft orange glass

Use when the user explicitly asks for a translucent, modern glass aesthetic. This is a proposed art direction inspired by that request, not an official Danang Dragons brand guideline or an Apple product endorsement.

## Palette

- Warm paper: `#FFF8F1`
- Pale apricot glass: `#F8C6A7` at roughly 18–35% opacity
- Dragon orange accent: `#F36B32` (use as a proposed accent; check the current approved team jersey/logo before calling it official)
- Ink navy: `#111827`
- Smoke gray: `#667085`
- Frost highlight: white at 35–55% opacity

Keep large fields warm and quiet. Use orange sparingly for the action, key metric, or light source. Add a dark version only when photographic contrast or the slide story needs it.

## Material treatment

- Use 2–4 glass moments across a short deck, not a panel on every slide.
- Build the effect with a soft tinted fill, restrained transparency, thin white highlight, gentle edge/shadow, and a faint orange glow. Keep blur behind the panel rather than on text.
- Use a single large pane, curved ribbon, or cropped lens shape to direct the eye. Do not stack many pills, cards, and rounded rectangles.
- Keep text on opaque or nearly opaque surfaces when the background is busy. Avoid tiny text, low-contrast translucent labels, excessive gradients, and decorative interface chrome.
- Use an editorial grid with generous margins and asymmetry. Let photos and typography carry most of the composition.

## Team and local cues

Use an approved current logo and player imagery only when supplied or officially licensed. A river-current line, basketball arc, warm arena reflections, city shoreline, or abstract dragon-scale texture can give the deck a Da Nang character without fabricating a team mark or event photograph.

## Typography and diagrams

Use a modern sans serif that renders Vietnamese diacritics correctly. One display face plus one text family at most. Use a strong headline, concise body copy, and visible data/source labels. Keep charts flat and direct; no glass panel may obscure a scale, value, axis, or source.
