# Visual Concept Lab

Create a visual idea that belongs to this specific brief. Use this skill for decks where the concept and imagery must persuade, not merely decorate.

## Workflow

1. Write the audience insight and desired response in one sentence each.
2. Generate three distinct creative territories. Each territory must include a short name, central metaphor, campaign line, hero image, and how it could become a real activation.
3. Select the strongest territory using relevance, originality, feasibility, proof, and visual potential. Explain the choice briefly; do not make the user do concept selection unless it changes the business direction.
4. Write one visual brief for each key image: subject, action, setting, composition, lighting, palette, camera/viewpoint, negative space for slide text, and what must not appear.
5. Map the images and diagrams to a storyboard. Give every visual a specific communication job.
6. Use approved photography for actual teams, players, uniforms, locations, and products. Use generated images for conceptual atmosphere, food still life, abstract form, or non-identifiable figures only.
7. Place supplied logos and final copy separately as editable slide elements. Do not ask an image model to render lettering, numbers, jersey text, or brand marks.

## Originality checks

- Replace generic stock tropes with a visual derived from the team, city, sponsor category, ritual, or local community.
- Avoid a deck where every slide is a centered headline over a dark background with one rectangular photo.
- Vary the composition: hero image, documentary frame, top-down layout, court/route diagram, close-up, short comparison, rights matrix, and measurement slide as content requires.
- Use one ownable visual motif throughout, such as a ball arc, river current, dragon-scale texture, or a light trail, but do not let the motif become a logo imitation or overwhelm evidence.
- Never pass off generated imagery as a real player, real game, or approved sponsor activation.

## Example territory for an F&B sponsor

“From first prep to final buzzer”: follow one repeatable team meal ritual into training, game day, and a fan reward. This is a creative starting point only. Verify team routines, player consent, sponsor rights, venue rules, products, and measurable actions before presenting them as commitments.
